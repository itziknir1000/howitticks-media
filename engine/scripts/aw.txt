Why are airplane windows never square?
Every flight, the cabin is pumped full of air, like a balloon.
The skin stretches, and the stress flows around every window.
Around a curve, it flows smoothly.
At a sharp corner, it piles up in one tiny spot.
Flight after flight, that spot cracks. Metal fatigue.
In nineteen fifty-four, two of the first jet airliners broke apart in mid-air.
The crack started at a window corner.
That curve isn't style. It's what holds the plane together.
